Description:- 
FORMULA HARDWARE is an open-theme hardware hackathon designed to encourage creativity, practical 
engineering, innovation and working prototypes. Teams may select any project idea, real-world problem 
statement, application area or technical domain, subject to safety and event guidelines. 
The event is inspired by the precision, pressure and teamwork of Formula 1, while focusing on real 
hardware, technical evaluation and practical implementation. 
 
 
01  EVENT OVERVIEW 
 Participants may bring any project idea or problem statement of their choice. 
 Projects may involve electronics, embedded systems, programming, mechanics, AI/ML, IoT, 
automation, robotics, communication, sustainability or social innovation. 
 No compulsory common hardware theme will be imposed. 
 The event has no racetrack, lap race, car-building requirement or compulsory racing concept. 
 Teams will be evaluated on technical clarity, feasibility, implementation, demonstration, teamwork 
and presentation. 
 
02  EVENT FORMAT 
 
ROUND 1 ROUND 2 
6-slide PPT 
Technical clarity & feasibility 
12- Hour Prototype Build 
Build • Test • Integrate • Demonstrate 
 
03  ROUND 1 — IDEA PRESENTATION 
Shortlisted teams will first present their proposed project idea to the evaluation panel. 
 Submit a 6-slide PowerPoint presentation. 
 The PPT must clearly explain the problem statement, proposed solution, working principle and expected 
outcome. 
 Teams should mention the major hardware, software, sensors, controllers, actuators or other resources 
planned. 
 Shortlisting will be based on technical clarity and feasibility. 
 Only shortlisted teams will proceed to Round 2. 
 Teams should be prepared to answer questions from judges regarding practicality, implementation and 
limitations.

Suggested PPT structure 
 Slide 1: Problem statement and motivation 
 Slide 2: Proposed solution and innovation 
 Slide 3: System architecture / working principle 
 Slide 4: Components and technical requirements 
 Slide 5: Feasibility, implementation plan and testing 
 Slide 6: Expected outcome, impact and future scope 
 
 
04  ROUND 2 — 12-HOUR PROTOTYPE BUILD & EVALUATION 
Shortlisted teams will develop, assemble, code, test and demonstrate a working hardware prototype during 
the 12-hour event window. 
 Event duration: 12 hours, from 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM. 
 Teams must bring their own components, tools, wires, consumables, batteries and project-specific 
materials. 
 Teams may build a new project or improve an existing concept, but prior work must be disclosed when 
requested. 
 Judges may ask for architecture explanation, live demonstration, subsystem testing and technical 
justification. 
 Partially functional projects may demonstrate working subsystems with an honest explanation of 
limitations.

Special Challenges 
 SURPRISE CONSTRAINT TWIST 
Teams may be given an unexpected technical or resource constraint during the building. They must 
adapt their design while maintaining safety and engineering logic. 
 REVERSE ENGINEERING CHALLENGE 
Teams may be asked to inspect, interpret or explain an assigned circuit, module, mechanism, signal 
path or system behavior. 
 BONUS MARKS 
Additional marks may be awarded for clearly explaining system logic, signal flow, fault detection, 
failure handling, debugging approach and design trade-offs. 
 
Round 2 evaluation focus: 
 Working performance and live demonstration 
 Technical architecture and integration 
 Implementation quality, neatness and reliability 
 Testing, debugging and validation 
 Response to surprise constraints 
 Reverse engineering understanding 
 Teamwork, communication and technical presentation 
 
05  ELIGIBILITY & TEAM FORMATION 
 The event is open to engineering students. 
 Each team must consist of 2 to 5 members. 
 Each member must have a clearly defined responsibility. 
 Each team must nominate one Team Captain for official communication. 
 All members are expected to contribute meaningfully and understand the project. 
 
06  COMPONENTS, TOOLS & RESOURCES 
 Every team is responsible for bringing its own components, modules, sensors, controllers, motors, 
batteries, mechanical parts, tools, wires, consumables and other project requirements. 
 Organizers will not provide project-specific components unless announced before the event. 
 Teams must verify component compatibility, ratings, battery condition and safe operation. 
 Use of pre-built modules is permitted as part of a larger original system. 
 Presenting a ready-made complete project as original work is prohibited. 
 Any external assistance, online resources or AI tool used must be disclosed when requested.

07  SAFETY & ETHICAL ENGINEERING 
 Follow safe soldering, wiring, battery handling, charging and power-use practices. 
 All electrical connections must be insulated, secure and suitable for operating current. 
 Battery terminals must be protected against short circuits, overheating and physical damage. 
 Projects must not contain sharp, exposed, loose, explosive or intentionally hazardous parts. 
 Organizers may stop or remove any unsafe project. 
 Plagiarism, false claims, fabricated results or misrepresentation may lead to disqualification.

 Teams must follow venue rules and directions from organizers, judges, mentors and volunteers.
